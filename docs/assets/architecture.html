Two-Stage Recommender — Architecture

Stage 1 retrieves candidates with matrix-factorization embeddings; stage 2 re-ranks them with a LightGBM model on richer features. Evaluated offline on a leave-last-out split against a popularity baseline, so each stage's lift is explicit.

Why real recommenders are two-stage

  • • retrieval narrows millions of items to a few hundred cheaply, and ranking spends a heavier model only on those
  • • stage 1 is Truncated-SVD user/item embeddings scoring every item and keeping the top-N: cheap, high recall
  • • stage 2 is a LightGBM classifier re-scoring those candidates on svd-score, popularity, category affinity, user activity and item quality
  • • most junior portfolios stop at cosine-similarity top-k; the point here is doing the full pipeline AND measuring each stage's contribution

The eval is leave-last-out, so nothing leaks

  • • each user's most recent interaction is held out as the test positive and never appears in training
  • • scored with Recall@k, NDCG@k and MAP@k over 2000 test users at k=10, against a popularity baseline that both stages have to clear

The numbers are reported straight, not cherry-picked

  • • both personalized systems crush the popularity baseline: SVD retrieval at about 1.9× Recall@10, the two-stage system at about 1.4×
  • • but the learned ranker did NOT beat single-stage retrieval here (0.1065 against 0.1405), and that is reported rather than hidden
  • • the honest read: on this synthetic co-occurrence-only dataset the SVD signal is so strong that retrieval is near-optimal and the ranker has nothing left to add
  • • stage 2 pays off in production where it sees features retrieval cannot: price, recency, live CTR, margin, context
